This quarter we covered the core of React: components, props, state, events, effects, fetching data, and deploying. That is enough to build real apps, and it is a small slice of what the React and full-stack world has to offer. This project is your chance to explore one new piece of it and teach the rest of us what you found.

## What You'll Do

With your partner, pick a React or full-stack topic we have not covered, build a small demo that shows it working, and present it to the class in 5 to 10 minutes.

Everyone presents on the same day. With around ten groups, that is a class full of short, focused demos. By the end you will each have seen a dozen new tools you can reach for in your final project and beyond. That is the real payoff. You learn one thing deeply by building it, and you pick up eleven more quickly by watching everyone else.

## The Format

Your presentation covers three things:

1. **What it is.** The one or two sentence version of the topic.
2. **Why and when you'd use it.** The problem it solves. What was annoying or impossible before, and what this makes easy.
3. **A live demo.** Show it actually working. This is the part people remember.

Keep it to 5 to 10 minutes. Short and clear wins here.

## Claiming a Topic

We claim topics on the whiteboard at the start of class so no two groups present the same thing. Write your names and your topic on the board. First come, first served. If the topic you wanted is already up there, pick another. Claiming early is worth it because the popular topics go fast.

The next page has a list of suggested topics to get you started. You are also welcome to pick any React or full-stack topic that interests you and that we have not already covered.

> **With your partner:** Before you look at the list, brainstorm for two minutes. Is there something you have seen in a real app and wondered how it works? That curiosity often makes a better topic than anything on a list, because you are already motivated to figure it out.
