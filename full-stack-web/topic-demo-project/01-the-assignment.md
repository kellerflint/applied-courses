This quarter we covered the core of React: components, props, state, events, effects, fetching data, and deploying. That is enough to build real apps, but only a slice of what the React and full-stack world has to offer. This project is your chance to explore a new piece and teach the rest of us about what you found.

## What You'll Do

With your partner, pick a React or full-stack topic we have not covered, build a small demo that shows it working, and present it to the class in 5 to 10 minutes. By the end you will each have seen a dozen new tools you can reach for in your final project and beyond.

## The Format

Your presentation covers:

1. **What it is.** What is your topic? Introduce it.
2. **Why and when you'd use it.** The problem it solves. What was annoying or impossible before, and what this makes easy.
3. **A live demo.** Show it actually working. This is the part people remember.
4. **Code explaination.** Show us how you actually set it up. Teach us how it works.

Keep it to 5 to 10 minutes.

## Claiming a Topic

Claim your topic on the whiteboard at the start of class so no two groups present the same thing. Write your names and your topic on the board. First come, first serve. If the topic you wanted is already up there, pick another.

The next page has a list of suggested topics to get you started. You are also welcome to pick any React or full-stack topic that interests you and that we have not already covered.

> **With your partner:** Before you look at the list, brainstorm for two minutes. Is there something you have seen in a real app and wondered how it works? Following that curiosity often makes for a better topic.
